There's a kind of freedom you only find inside a tight box.

I spent a week trying to design this portfolio with every option open — any framework, any aesthetic, any layout. The result was paralysis: fourteen half-finished prototypes and a growing suspicion that maximum freedom is just maximum surface area for anxiety.

So I gave myself a brief. One accent colour. Two fonts. A dot grid that could only do one thing well. No gradients, no shadows on text, no stock photography.

## The brief as creative act

Constraints force sequence. When you can't reach for a third typeface, you learn what the two you have are actually capable of. IBM Plex Mono at 9.5px with 0.3em tracking reads nothing like IBM Plex Mono at 16px — and you only discover that when *another weight* isn't on the table.

The vermilion rule was the hardest. One colour, used once per context. Every time I wanted to reach for it a second time on a page, I had to ask: *which use is the real one?* That question alone tightened the work more than any style guide could.

## What ships

The dot grid ended up being the load-bearing creative element precisely because it was constrained to a single canvas. No colour fills, no gradients — just 2,300 monochrome points that had to carry a face, a venn diagram, a graph-paper texture, and the sense that something was *alive* on the screen.

When you can't buy interest cheaply, you earn it structurally.

## Try it

Pick one constraint for your next thing. Not a tool constraint (*only Vim*) — a constraint on the output. One typeface. One hue. One interaction pattern. Watch what you make when there's nowhere to hide.
